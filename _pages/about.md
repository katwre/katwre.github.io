I'm a Zurich-based data scientist with 9+ years of experience spanning machine learning, biomedical research, and software development.

I studied computer science and bioinformatics at the [University of Warsaw’s Faculty of Mathematics, Informatics and Mechanics (MIMUW)](https://www.mimuw.edu.pl/en/) and completed a Ph.D. in computational molecular biology with Prof. Altuna Akalin’s group at the [Bioinformatics and Omics Data Science Platform at MDC-BIMSB](https://www.mdc-berlin.de/bioinformatics). There, I co-developed the BioC [genomation](https://github.com/BIMSBbioinfo/genomation) package and the [PiGx BS-seq](https://github.com/BIMSBbioinfo/pigx) snakemake-based workflow, explored [phantom peaks in ChIP-seq](https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkz460) and DNA-methylation changes in neuroblastoma and [cardiovascular disease](https://doi.org/10.1093/nargab/lqad061). Later, at ETH Zurich ([Laboratory of Nutrition and Metabolic Epigenetics](https://epigenetics.ethz.ch/), [Laboratory of Regenerative and Muscle Biology](https://rmb.ethz.ch/)) and the [Functional Genomics Center Zurich](https://fgcz.ch/the-center/people/rehrauer.html), I worked on multiple projects, including germline chimerism based on scRNA-seq.

In freelance and industry projects, I use machine learning and deep learning to turn complex biomedical and sensor data into biologically meaningful insights.  For water-safety monitoring, I built a PyTorch LSTM variational autoencoder to detect and forecast unusual patterns in high-frequency chemical measurements. In a menstrual-effluent biomarker project, I combined generative modeling and scArches-based transfer learning to analyze bulk and scRNA-seq, then evaluated classifiers for endometriosis-associated signatures. For oncology, I used positive-unlabeled learning and autoencoders to prioritize therapeutic targets from molecular and treatment data. As part of a team, I applied [Enformer](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-021-01252-x/), a published hybrid CNN–Transformer, to multi-omics data in Alzheimer’s disease research, identifying candidate regulatory enhancers and their potential target genes. Across these projects, I work closely with research, clinical, and data science teams to make analyses reproducible and useful for downstream decisions.
